## Intent
Give the suspense first (what is this pile?), then the answer (dozens of items that fly into the page and each take their place). The viewer should feel "things pouring in without end", with every card slamming into place with urgency.

## Motion core
- First half (pile-orbit-open, optional): all the cards are stacked into a physical pile with real height, placed on a dark brushed-graphite desktop beside the page board; the top light hits only the pile, and the camera orbits the pile in a side-angled close-up; as it pulls back the "light" lights up the dark page board from the pile with a soft-edged aperture - the page board is always a display board resting on a dark desk, and the dark surround runs through to the last frame
- **Anticipation beat (anticipation, borrowing Disney principle 2)**: give one segment-level beat of anticipation before the first card is dealt - the whole pile presses down slightly + the top card pulls back opposite to the dealing direction, so the viewer's eye is first led to the pile before watching the deal. Anticipation is done once at segment level, **not added per card** (per-card anticipation would drag down the hard-accelerating beat of R2)
- Second half (deck-deal): 26 cards are dealt from the pile to the grid target positions in reading order, with the dealing interval shrinking in hard acceleration; each card's flight has a z arc apex, an overshoot settle and a landing press rebound (settle/press is Principle 5's follow-through - a fast stop needs a cushion, and cutting it reads as a hard freeze)
- The camera chases the deal towards the board's tail (faster and faster) while rising, tilting back and turning sideways, and lands on a runway-style overhead: the whole board stretches along the diagonal into the distance, all 26 cards are in frame, and the far end is darkened for depth of field; once the board is full it holds still (> 0.5s, with only a very slow 1.5% push-in), and a slogan + mono counter line rises at the bottom left (the count jumps with the number of cards landed). In the closing pause the layers settle on different frames - the card bodies settle first, the shadow convergence lags by ~3f, and the afterimages dissipate last (drag hierarchy, borrowing Principle 5; all elements freezing on the same frame reads as mechanical)

| Anticipation beat | 10f before the first dealing cue (26→36): the whole pile presses down 48px (about sixty percent of the pile height) + the top card pulls back 30px opposite to the dealing direction, easing easeOut, released the instant the first card goes | **The amplitude must exceed the naked-eye threshold**: the initial 4px/2px was tested by the user as completely imperceptible, and only after enlarging 12 times (48/30px) was it ruled as passing (precedent 2026-07-09 evolution round #2) - after rendering, self-check "can you see the wind-up without going frame by frame"; if you cannot see it, it is as good as not done; the frame budget is taken from the tail of the orbit and does not squeeze the deal segment |
| Deal beat | Cue of card k = 36 + 4k − 0.0792·k(k−1), interval shrinking from 4f to 0.2f | A uniform interval immediately looks mechanical; a uniform acceleration that is not fierce enough was also rejected - it must be "hard-accelerating" level (R2/B3) |
| Card quantity | 10 real cards + 16 extras = 26 cards; the grid extends 5 rows downward, paper extended 2036px | Without enough cards the sense of inflow does not hold - better too many than too few; extras land in real extended grid positions and do not float (Q9) |
| Single-card flight | deal 8f (bezier(0.3,0,0.2,1), z arc apex +110px sin, scale peak 1.06) + settle 4f (bezier(0.3,0,0.25,1.15) slight overshoot) + press 0.996→1 rebound 2f | For the landing to bounce it needs a bezier with y1>1; settle+press total 6f ≈ 30% of the 8f flight - a fast stop with enough cushion (Principle 5 proportion); after settling, force an identity transform to prevent sub-pixel drift on the tilted board |
| Closing drag hierarchy | Settling in layers within the rest segment: the card body locks as soon as the press rebound ends, the landing shadow `0 32px→0 2px` converges 2-4f behind the card body, and the flight afterimage's opacity tail drags on another 3-5f before dissipating | Borrowing the drag hierarchy of Principle 5 (root → primary → secondary offset by 2-4f); the 0.5s rest stillness precedent is unchanged - the layer offset happens at the head of the rest segment and everything is locked dead by the end of rest (the pattern has been distilled into assets/lib/helpers/motion.ts lagged) |
| Motion blur (single card) | In flight, a 5% path-lagging blur(6px) afterimage, opacity 0.25·(1−t) | A ghost afterimage is cheaper than true motion blur and sufficient - keep this approach at single-card level |
| Motion blur (camera) | For fast camera-move segments of ≥30px/f such as the chasing scroll, wrap the shot with `@remotion/motion-blur`'s `<CameraMotionBlur shutterAngle={180} samples={10}>`; use `<Sequence>` to cut the wrapped range to the start of the fast segment, and do not wrap the slow close-up segment (<10px/f) | At 30fps a camera jumping 50-70px per frame strobes, and the trailing of a fast pan is the physical instinct of a cinema camera; the larger the shutterAngle the longer the trail (>270 smears), and samples is tuned to the render budget (each +1 is about +1x render time); wrapping the whole shot would soften the sampling of the 1px-level fine texture (brushed metal) in the slow orbit segment - wrap only the fast segment; borrowed from the official Remotion ecosystem, not a user precedent (2026-07-11 evolution round #8) |

## Sound
Pin whoosh-big to the orbit pull-back + first batch of deals (the template film pins it at f308), and pin whoosh-fast to the deal acceleration segment (f340/356). Three tricks to avoid a machine-gun feel in consecutive landing sounds: alternate two samples, step the volume down, and let the interval follow the animation curve's acceleration - when it gets so dense it smears into one piece, fade the sound out into a single swoosh and do not score card by card (S2).

## Known pitfalls
- An abstract flood-style approach failed to converge over many rounds; switching to the "dealing cards" physical metaphor passed in one round (R2/B3) - for group motion find a physical metaphor first, then write code
- Card textures must be real page-element slices (Q1); hand-built fake cards look fake at a glance; extras reuse real card slices placed with offsets
- Everything flying at the same time reads as an explosion; staggered, increasingly accelerating timing is the soul; if the first-half orbit is cut, the deal's starting point needs another explanation of "where the pile came from"
- The anticipation/follow-through technique is borrowed from Disney's 12 principles (the distilled version built into the pixel2motion skill); the anticipation amplitude already has a user precedent (2026-07-09: "OK I can see it now", after two earlier small amplitudes both went unnoticed); drag-hierarchy frame offsets remain the default suggestion - when in conflict with the R2 precedent (e.g. the user asks for faster and harder), the precedent wins
- The textbook proportion (anticipation = 20-30% of the main action) yields too small a pixel displacement at a pulled-back camera position - **compute pixels before proportions**, since promo-film viewers watch the wide shot, not the close-up
- `<Sequence>` rebases frame numbers + CameraMotionBlur's internal Freeze makes PageCam sample the wrong frame (horizontal mis-blur breaks the illusion) - for shots choreographed in absolute frames, pass PageCam the `frame` prop to restore the absolute frame (the lib component already supports it)
