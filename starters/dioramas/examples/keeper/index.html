Take the light.

Somewhere out there a boat has lost the coast. The lamp is yours now: aim the beam, find her, and hold it on her for two seconds. She'll find her way in.

Since 1861 the lamp on Sgeir Dubh has been lit every night of the year. It has never once been late.

    02 The rooms

    Four rooms, stacked up a tower.

    I Ground floor · Sleeps 2 · 24 m²

    Keeper’s Quarters

    The cottage the keepers lived in for a hundred and forty years. A peat stove, a bed built into the wall, and a window the sea comes right up to on a spring tide. The original log books are on the shelf. Read them.

    • Peat stove
    • Box bed
    • Log books, 1861–2002

    II Seaward end · Sleeps 2 · 30 m²

    The Fog Signal

    The old engine house for the horn, which still sounds at noon on the first Monday of the month. A copper bath under the horn’s brass mouth, walls a metre thick, and a window at the height of the surf.

    • Copper bath
    • The horn still works
    • Surf-level window

    III Level five · Sleeps 2 · 19 m²

    The Watch

    The room where the keeper stayed awake. Six small windows all the way round, a brass telescope, and a chart of every wreck on this reef. It is the first room on the rock to catch the dawn.

    • Windows all round
    • Brass telescope
    • Wreck charts

    IV The top · Sleeps 2 · 16 m² · 118 steps

    The Lamp Room

    You sleep beside the lens. It turns all night, because it’s a working light, and a blackout curtain rings the bed. Almost everyone leaves it open.

    • Beside a working lens
    • Glass on every side
    • No signal, anywhere

    03 The crossing

    Nine miles, weather permitting.

    The Morag Ann leaves Ardcarra Quay at ten on Tuesdays and Fridays. It is fifty-eight minutes across if the sea agrees. The keeper makes the call at seven each morning, and the keeper’s call is final.

    04 Dawn

    And then, the morning.

    At sunrise the keeper puts out the light. The sea goes flat and pink, the gannets come back, and you realise how far away everything is.
