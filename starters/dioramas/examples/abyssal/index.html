    Challenger Deep · 11°22′N 142°35′E · three guests per dive

    The last unexplored place on Earth is 11 km down.

    Abyssal takes three guests at a time to the deepest point of the ocean, in a titanium submersible built for nothing else. The round trip is nine hours. Four of them are spent on the floor.

    Sunlight Epipelagic

    The first two hundred metres hold most of the ocean's life and all of its sunlight. Pelagia leaves the A-frame at 06:40 and falls through it in three minutes. Look up while you can: the surface is a moving ceiling of light, and you will not see anything like it for the next nine hours.

    Twilight Mesopelagic

    Red is the first colour the water takes from you. By 300 m Pelagia's orange hull reads as wet slate; by 600 m everything is one shade of blue. This is where animals start making their own light. At 750 m the floodlights come on, and colour comes back with them.

    Midnight Bathypelagic

    No sunlight has ever reached this water. The only light down here is made by the living, and most of it is bait. An anglerfish can wait months for a meal; she lets the lure do the hunting. Keep the floodlights moving. She is closer than she looks.

    Zone 04 / 054,000 — 6,000 m

    Abyss Abyssopelagic

    The abyssal plain looks empty until it doesn't. On the trench lip at 4,970 m a black smoker vents water at 380 °C, kept liquid by the pressure. Around it grow tube worms with no mouth and no gut, fed by bacteria that eat sulphur. Life, it turns out, never needed the sun.

    Zone 05 / 056,000 — 10,935 m

    Hadal Hadopelagic

    Named for Hades. Until 2019, more people had walked on the Moon than had stood here. The pressure is over seven tonnes on every square inch of the sphere, and the silt is so fine it hangs in the lights like smoke. We touch down beside Beacon 07, still pinging on the spot where we first landed.
